Three design directions

Each mockup is a complete home page plus a specimen of the interior "rule page" template — the layout roughly eighty pages will share. Copy is drawn from the actual manuscript, not placeholder text, so the client can judge the design against real content. The directions differ in layout and posture, not only in palette.

A · Heritage

Navy · Brass · Bone

Serif headlines on a formal grid, hairline rules, a numbered chapter structure. Reads like a private bank or a university endowment: permanent, fiduciary, generational.

Best for
Grandparents, high-net-worth families, financial advisors. Leans into the estate-planning material and the 30th anniversary.
Risk
Can feel exclusive to a 32-year-old opening a first account.
Open mockup →

B · Evergreen

Forest · Sand · Amber

Rounded sans, soft cards, generous whitespace, an onboarding quiz in the hero. Growth and family without the dollar-bill-green cliché. Myths, examples, and tools lead.

Best for
Closing the 54% awareness gap — parents who find finance intimidating.
Risk
Softness must be balanced with authority signals or it reads as a blog.
Open mockup →

C · Almanac

Ink · Vermilion · Slate

An editorial reference handbook. Numbered rules, dense typography, marginalia, real data tables. Fastest to scan and the cheapest system to scale across 150 pages.

Best for
Becoming the 529 reference the community links to and searches.
Risk
Least "designed" at first glance — sells on use, not on a screenshot.
Open mockup →

Recommendation

Almanac for structure, Evergreen for warmth. The Almanac's typographic system and information density are the right bones for 150 rule pages that people arrive at from search. Evergreen's palette, example cards, and onboarding are what make the site inviting to the audience that doesn't yet know 529s exist. A hybrid — Almanac layout, Evergreen palette — is a legitimate fourth option worth putting to the client alongside these three.

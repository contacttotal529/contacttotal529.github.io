Chapter IV · Using It for K–12

You may pay for a tutor through a 529 account.

Since the 2025 legislation, qualified expenses include tuition for tutoring or educational classes taken outside the home — including at a tutoring facility — provided the tutor is not related to the student and is licensed as a teacher in any state, has taught at an eligible educational institution, or is a subject-matter expert in the relevant subject.

Your state may differ

Thirteen states do not allow tax-free K–12 distributions at all. California applies an additional 2.5% penalty on withdrawals taken for this purpose.

Paul is a former spelling-bee champion who wants his grandchildren to do well at English. He pays for private spelling lessons for his four grandchildren at an expert's home. The unrelated teacher signs a note confirming subject-matter expertise and individual instruction. Paul keeps records in case of an audit or a change in IRS guidance.

Grandparent · K–12 · Recordkeeping
Decision tree — what counts as a "tutor"?
  1. Is it a subject in which the student might one day earn a scholarship?
  2. Is the instruction one-on-one or in a small group, focused on that subject?
  3. Is the instructor unrelated to the student and located outside your home?
  4. Are you willing to change course as further IRS guidance arrives?

A math, English, or Spanish after-school tutor outside the home should qualify. A violin teacher giving personalized one-on-one instruction functions similarly. A dance teacher leading large studio classes likely does not. There is no IRS case guidance yet — keep receipts, and have instructors sign a short attestation.
